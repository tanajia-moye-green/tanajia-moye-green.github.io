I study the economic impact of incarceration on families, with a focus on how loved ones — particularly partners — navigate financial strain, fines and fees, and the broader challenges of supporting justice-impacted individuals. I'm also interested in how the consequences of maternal vs. paternal incarceration differ in shaping child and family well-being. I am a mixed-methods sociologist with a current emphasis on quantitative methods — a reflection of both my training and my disability, which I name openly as part of who I am as a scholar. I believe deeply in the importance of being accountable to and engaged within our local communities, not just the academy. A proud Floridian committed to the critical work being done in and for the South. Knight-Hennessy Scholar, NSF Graduate Research Fellow, and Fulbright Scholar.

Research
How Mothers' Income Packaging Responds to Partner Incarceration
Using latent class analysis on FFCWS data to examine how women's economic survival strategies shift when a partner is incarcerated — with a central intersectional finding about Black mothers with some college education.
In progress
The Racialized Spillover Effects of Mass Incarceration for Women
County-year panel analysis with two-way fixed effects examining how male incarceration rates shape female poverty across racial groups, with lagged effects as a key finding.
In progress
The Costs of Justice: Fines and Fees in Florida
Assessing the legitimacy of legal financial obligations through utilitarian and retributivist frameworks, with an intersectional lens exposing how both assume a fictional universal legal subject.
In progress
Epistemic Devaluation & Sociological Knowledge
A two-paper project on how sociological knowledge gets dismissed and how marginalized communities can engage in epistemic resistance — reframing intersectionality as always having been about model misspecification.

Methods & Data Skills
Latent Class Analysis (LCA)
Including BCH correction for distal outcomes. Used to identify heterogeneous subgroups in economic survival strategies among mothers with incarcerated partners.
Two-Way Fixed Effects Panel Models
County-year panel designs controlling for unobserved heterogeneity across units and time. Applied to Vera Institute data (2009–2019) examining racialized spillover effects of incarceration.
Causal Inference & Quasi-Experimental Design
Transition-based quasi-experimental designs and propensity score matching for robustness. Experienced in designing studies that approximate causal claims with observational data.
Multinomial Regression
With clustered standard errors for nested and grouped data structures. Used alongside LCA to examine predictors of class membership and economic outcomes.
Administrative Data & Web Scraping
Large-scale PDF scraping and coding of court records and government documents in R. Undergraduate honors thesis examined group threat and punitiveness using scraped and merged state-level datasets.
Survey & Secondary Data Analysis
Experienced with FFCWS, Vera Institute administrative court data, and national secondary datasets. Proficient in R for data cleaning, merging, analysis, and visualization.
I am a mixed-methods sociologist with deep quantitative training. My current research program emphasizes quantitative secondary data designs — a deliberate methodological choice informed in part by my disability. I am always interested in methodological questions and welcome conversations about research design.

Consulting & Research Services

Rigorous quantitative analysis, rooted in understanding and depicting people's lived experiences — for organizations working at the intersection of justice, policy, and inequality. I help organizations move from messy data to rigorous, actionable findings without losing sight of the communities those findings are about.
